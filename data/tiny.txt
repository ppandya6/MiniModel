The little machine watched the rain fall on the garden. Each drop made a circle, and each circle became a story.
A scientist opened a notebook and asked a simple question. Could a small machine learn the patterns of a language?
The first experiment began with letters. Letters formed words, and words formed sentences. The machine guessed the next letter.
Some guesses were right. Some guesses were wrong. With every mistake, the scientist adjusted the weights and tried again.
In the morning the garden was quiet. A bird sang near the window while the machine learned another line of text.
The scientist compared two models. One was narrow and quick. The other was wide and slow. Both had something to teach.
Attention helped the machine remember earlier letters. Position helped it distinguish the beginning from the end.
A notebook held the results of every experiment. The scientist measured the loss on text the machine had never studied.
Learning was not the same as remembering. A model could repeat a story and still fail to understand a new sentence.
The small machine made a new story about a bird in a rainy garden. It was imperfect, but its patterns were becoming clear.
A fair experiment changes one thing at a time. A careful scientist records the seed, the data, and the time spent learning.
The garden grew through sunshine and rain. The model improved through examples and mistakes. Neither grew in a single day.
